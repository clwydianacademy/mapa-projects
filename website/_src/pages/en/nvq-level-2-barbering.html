The fee

What the fee buys — and what it is not

Training in this trade is sold in three shapes, and they are priced in three different worlds.

A one-day attendance certificate teaches a technique and gives you a document an insurer will accept. It carries no regulated credit and it does not make anyone qualified. It is the right purchase for a stylist adding a service, and it costs what a day costs.

A single regulated unit — the kind we run on the CPD calendar from {{cpd:minPrice}} to {{cpd:maxPrice}} — is assessed, externally quality assured, and counts toward a Diploma as one of its units. It proves competence in one area of practice. It does not, on its own, make someone a hairdresser.

A full NVQ Level 2 Diploma is the qualification the trade means when it says “qualified”. It is the whole occupational standard — cutting, beard work, shaving, consultation, health and safety, working to appointment pressure — assessed across every unit on live clients, and it is what an employer, an insurer or a chair-rental landlord is asking for when they ask if you are qualified. That is what {{fee:nvq}} buys here.

What is inside it

  • The full Diploma, not a certificate in one area of practice — every mandatory unit, assessed on live models rather than blocks
  • {{fee:barberingWeeks}} weeks, one evening a week, because the theory is timetabled as distance learning and every academy evening is hands-on
  • The Commercial Resilience Module — pricing, retention, digital booking, and reading your own numbers. {{stat:profit:Clause}}, and {{stat:self_employed:clause}}. Technical skill alone does not survive that
  • Three specialist add-on units most entry courses skip: an introduction to luxury wet shaving, the business of barbering, and retail product promotion
  • Continuous assessment into a digital portfolio, with independent internal quality assurance — no single exam day that ends the course
  • {{fee:SeatsWord}} seats. Not a class of thirty with one tutor and a rota for the basins

And what it actually costs you

{{fee:nvq}} is the self-funded price, payable as {{fee:deposit}} and then {{fee:instalment}} a month for {{fee:instalmentsWord}} months. On a funded route or an apprenticeship it is £0 — the apprentice earns while they qualify and the salon pays wages only.

Set against a two-year college route, the question is not which price is lower. It is how many months you spend not yet earning as a qualified stylist, and what you can charge at the end.
