Training your salon team in North Wales: the routes, the costs, and the trade-offs

There are four ways to get a member of your team trained, and they suit different situations. This sets them out neutrally — including the ones we do not sell — because the wrong route costs you a stylist, and an owner choosing well is worth more to us than an owner talked into the wrong thing.

Route one: take on an apprentice

What it is. You employ someone, they train while working, and the Welsh apprenticeship framework funds the training in full. You pay wages.

Best for: building someone from scratch into the way your salon works, and filling a chair you expect to need for years.

The trade-off: it is the longest route — typically eighteen months to two years per level — and you are employing someone who is not yet productive while they learn. Training providers deliver this free to you, which makes it unbeatable on price and slow on time.

Route two: send an existing member of staff for a regulated unit

What it is. One person, one day a week, for between {{cpd:minDays}} and {{cpd:maxDays}} weeks depending on the unit, coming back with regulated credit in a named technique.

Best for: a stylist who can already cut and colour and needs a service line you are currently turning away — extensions, relaxing, colour correction, perming.

The trade-off: you pay, and you lose them from the floor for that day. Our prices run {{cpd:minPrice}} to {{cpd:maxPrice}} depending on the unit’s guided learning hours, on a published formula, with team rates from {{cpd:tierFromWord:1}} people upward.

Route three: a short attendance course

What it is. A day or two, a technique, a certificate your insurer will usually accept.

Best for: speed and a specific branded system. If you need it for next month, this is the route.

The trade-off: no regulated credit, nothing banked toward a qualification, and the standard is the provider’s own.

Route four: train them in-house

What it is. Your senior stylist teaches your junior.

Best for: the way your salon does things, which nobody else can teach.

The trade-off: it is the most expensive option in the room and the easiest to miscount. Your senior stylist’s hour is your most valuable hour, two columns stop earning at once, and there is no qualification at the end of it. It works best alongside a formal route, not instead of one.

Practical things owners ask us

How do I cover someone who is training?

One person, one day a week. Our shortest unit is a single day; the longest is {{cpd:daysWord:UNHB374}}. Every date for the term is published in advance so you can build the rota once.

Can I send two people without losing both on the same day?

Yes. We run the same units on {{cpd:strandDays}}. Most salons close on a Monday, some cannot, and a salon sending two people can send them on different days rather than losing both at once.

How do team rates work?

Team rates count people from your salon across the whole term, not bookings on one date, so one stylist on colour and two on extensions already reaches the first tier. One invoice.

Where does the money go?

Our prices come from a published formula, the same one for everybody. There is no negotiated rate you missed out on.
