The fee

What the fee buys — and what it is not

Training in this trade is sold in three shapes, and they are priced in three different worlds.

A one-day attendance certificate teaches a technique and gives you a document an insurer will accept. It carries no regulated credit and it does not make anyone qualified. It is the right purchase for a stylist adding a service, and it costs what a day costs.

A single regulated unit — the kind we run on the CPD calendar from £195 to £835 — is assessed, externally quality assured, and counts toward a Diploma as one of its units. It proves competence in one area of practice. It does not, on its own, make someone a hairdresser.

A full NVQ Level 2 Diploma is the qualification the trade means when it says “qualified”. It is the whole occupational standard — cutting, colouring, styling, consultation, health and safety, working to appointment pressure — assessed across every unit on live clients, and it is what an employer, an insurer or a chair-rental landlord is asking for when they ask if you are qualified. That is what £4,500 buys here.

What is inside it

  • The full Diploma, not a certificate in one area of practice — every mandatory unit, assessed on live models rather than blocks
  • Six months, not two years, because the theory is timetabled as distance learning and every academy day is hands-on
  • The Commercial Resilience Module — pricing, retention, digital booking, and reading your own numbers. 60% of high-street salons are no longer turning a net profit under rising costs and tax burdens, and 60.5% of UK hairdressing professionals now work self-employed. Technical skill alone does not survive that
  • Elite add-on units most courses leave out: modern perming, prestige extensions, chemical relaxing and smoothing
  • Continuous assessment into a digital portfolio, with independent internal quality assurance — no single exam day that ends the course
  • Ten seats. Not a class of thirty with one tutor and a rota for the basins

And what it actually costs you

£4,500 is the self-funded price, payable as £500 and then £500 a month for eight months. On a funded route or an apprenticeship it is £0 — the apprentice earns while they qualify and the salon pays wages only.

Set against a two-year college route, the question is not which price is lower. It is how many months you spend not yet earning as a qualified stylist, and what you can charge at the end.

Compare the fee to another fee and you will compare the wrong things. Compare what you hold at the end.
